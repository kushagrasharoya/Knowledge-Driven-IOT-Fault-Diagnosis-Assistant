============================================================
INDUSTRIAL MACHINE MAINTENANCE KNOWLEDGE BASE
============================================================

TOPIC: HIGH PROCESS TEMPERATURE
Condition: Process temperature exceeds normal operating threshold (>= 312 K).
Possible Causes:
- Insufficient cooling or coolant flow failure
- Excessive friction in spindle or drive components
- Ambient temperature rise affecting heat dissipation
- Continuous heavy load operation
- Clogged heat exchanger or cooling filters

Inspection Steps:
1. Inspect the coolant reservoir level and coolant pump pressure.
2. Check heat exchanger and radiator filters for dust or debris.
3. Verify ambient air temperature and ventilation around the machine.
4. Measure spindle bearing temperature using an infrared thermometer.
5. Review machine load cycle and thermal dissipation efficiency.

Recommended Actions:
- Restore coolant levels or replace degraded cutting fluid.
- Clean or replace clogged cooling radiator filters.
- Reduce operating load cycle until temperature stabilizes below 310 K.
- Inspect coolant delivery nozzles for blockages.
- Schedule spindle bearing lubrication if localized friction is detected.


TOPIC: HIGH TORQUE
Condition: Spindle or motor torque exceeds safe operating limits (>= 55 Nm).
Possible Causes:
- Excessive mechanical load during cutting
- Increased mechanical friction in drive train or guideways
- Mechanical obstruction or binding
- Worn or chipped cutting tool components
- Tool-related resistance or incorrect feed rate

Inspection Steps:
1. Check whether the machine feed rate and depth of cut exceed workpiece specifications.
2. Inspect moving mechanical components (guideways, ball screws) for binding.
3. Check for excessive friction or lack of lubrication on sliding surfaces.
4. Inspect the tool and related cutting insert edges for micro-chipping or breakage.
5. Compare live torque with nominal baseline operating values.

Recommended Actions:
- Reduce excessive mechanical load and optimize feed rate.
- Inspect mechanical drive components for physical resistance.
- Inspect the cutting tool and replace it immediately if excessive wear is present.
- Clear any chips or mechanical obstruction from the work area.
- Monitor torque continuously after restarting the cycle.


TOPIC: HIGH TOOL WEAR
Condition: Cumulative tool wear exceeds replacement threshold (>= 150 min).
Possible Causes:
- Normal abrasive wear during prolonged cutting cycles
- Cutting tough or high-hardness materials with standard tooling
- Inadequate lubrication during cutting operations
- Thermal softening due to elevated cutting temperatures
- Tool life expired beyond manufacturer guidelines

Inspection Steps:
1. Stop the cycle and visually inspect the cutting tool edge using a magnifier.
2. Check flank wear, crater wear, and insert edge degradation.
3. Measure workpiece surface finish tolerance for degradation.
4. Review total continuous operating minutes on the active tool.
5. Check coolant concentration and delivery directly at the cutting zone.

Recommended Actions:
- Replace the worn cutting tool insert with a calibrated fresh insert.
- Reset the tool wear tracking timer in the machine controller.
- Verify tool offset calibrations and zero-point alignment.
- Ensure proper coolant flow directed at the tool-workpiece interface.
- Inspect workpiece quality to ensure tolerances remain within specifications.


TOPIC: LOW ROTATIONAL SPEED
Condition: Spindle rotational speed drops below normal operating range (< 1300 RPM).
Possible Causes:
- Heavy cutting load causing spindle motor stall or slip
- Variable frequency drive (VFD) fault or power fluctuation
- Belt slippage or drive coupling misalignment
- Bearing pre-seizure or severe mechanical resistance
- Spindle motor overload protection triggering current throttling

Inspection Steps:
1. Check VFD status codes and drive fault logs.
2. Inspect spindle drive belts and pulleys for tension and wear.
3. Disengage spindle drive and check spindle rotation by hand for resistance.
4. Measure input voltage and current to the spindle motor.
5. Verify whether workpiece depth of cut is stalling the spindle.

Recommended Actions:
- Reduce depth of cut and feed rate to eliminate spindle stall.
- Re-tension or replace worn spindle drive belts.
- Inspect spindle bearings for overheating or mechanical binding.
- Check VFD configuration parameters and electrical supply stability.
- Restart spindle after verifying electrical and mechanical clearance.


TOPIC: MACHINE FAILURE RISK
Condition: Elevated overall failure probability (>= 40% to 100%) detected by predictive ML model.
Possible Causes:
- Compound degradation across multiple mechanical/thermal subsystems
- Elevated temperature combined with high torque or excessive tool wear
- Imminent component breakdown under peak production strain
- Sensor telemetry deviations reflecting anomalous machine dynamics

Inspection Steps:
1. Perform an emergency pause or isolation of the machine cycle.
2. Execute a multi-point inspection: temperatures, spindle torque, tool wear, and vibration.
3. Inspect safety interlocks and review diagnostic telemetry stream.
4. Check error codes on the main CNC/PLC controller console.
5. Verify mechanical clearances before attempting manual jog.

Recommended Actions:
- Stop or isolate the machine immediately to prevent catastrophic tool/spindle damage.
- Perform root cause diagnosis on the flagged sensor anomalies.
- Replace identified worn consumables (tools, filters, seals).
- Perform a controlled dry-run test at reduced speed before resuming production.
- Log the incident in the predictive maintenance maintenance register.
